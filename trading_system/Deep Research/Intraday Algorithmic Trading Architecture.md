Architecting the Transition from Daily to Intraday AI-Driven Algorithmic Trading Systems1. The Paradigm Shift to Intraday ExecutionThe evolution of an automated algorithmic trading portfolio from a daily, end-of-day (EOD) rebalancing cycle to a continuous intraday execution schedule represents a fundamental paradigm shift in quantitative systems architecture. In a purely daily operational model, predictive engines, signal processing layers, and risk governance frameworks operate under the assumption of discrete, 24-hour information assimilation. This daily frequency offers the advantage of smoothed volatility, wherein the noise of market microstructure is largely filtered out by the closing auction. However, this approach inherently fails to capture continuous price discovery, intraday volatility clustering, and transient microstructure anomalies that offer lucrative, short-horizon alpha. Furthermore, relying on a single daily cron-job exposes the portfolio to catastrophic desynchronization if the execution fails, while simultaneously subjecting the capital base to unmanaged overnight gap risk.Transitioning a highly sophisticated pipeline—currently comprising time-series foundation models (Amazon Chronos), state-space signal generators (Kalman Filters), multi-armed bandit ensembling (Thompson Sampling), and asymmetric volatility forecasting (GJR-GARCH)—from a 1x/day frequency to an hourly (7x/day) or 15-minute (26x/day) frequency introduces exponentially scaled complexities. The transition is not a mere infrastructural adjustment of scheduling software; it requires a deep mathematical overhaul of the entire trading stack.Chief among the challenges introduced by higher execution frequencies is the severe degradation of the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) due to market microstructure noise. Intraday price action is heavily influenced by bid-ask bounce, institutional algorithmic execution, and fleeting liquidity vacuums, all of which can easily be misinterpreted as directional signals by naive models. Additionally, the non-stationarity of intraday reward distributions requires rapid recalibration of contextual bandits, the severe drag of transaction costs (slippage and spread crossing) necessitates the induction of mathematical "no-trade regions," and standard risk models mechanically fail to account for the deterministic diurnal patterns of intraday volatility. Finally, the structural dilemma of overnight gap risk versus the transaction costs of daily liquidation must be resolved through rigorous expectancy modeling.This comprehensive research report provides an exhaustive analysis of the architectural, predictive, and risk-management modifications required to upgrade the specified daily AI-driven trading engine to a continuous intraday frequency.2. Pre-Processing and Signal Isolation on Intraday DataBefore analyzing the predictive foundation models, the signal processing layer—specifically the Principal Component Analysis (PCA) orthogonalization and the Conditional Autoencoder—must be structurally adapted to handle high-frequency 15-minute or 1-hour bar data.In the daily architecture, the pca_orthogonalization.py script isolates uncorrelated alpha streams by calculating the covariance matrix of the raw signals and extracting the principal components. On a daily timeframe, covariance matrices are relatively stable. However, on a 15-minute timeframe, correlation structures break down rapidly due to asynchronous trading, lead-lag effects between highly liquid and thinly traded assets, and transient macroeconomic shocks. To function intraday, the PCA must transition to a dynamic, exponentially weighted rolling covariance matrix, allowing the orthogonalization process to adapt to shifting intraday correlations without being anchored by stale morning data.Similarly, the conditional_autoencoder.py utilized for non-linear feature extraction and anomaly detection faces significant challenges with intraday data. High-frequency time series exhibit low signal-to-noise ratios and strong non-stationarities. Raw 15-minute prices contain high levels of unconventional noise, including bid-ask bounce and execution algorithms slicing large orders. If raw intraday data is fed directly into the autoencoder, the neural network will likely expend its latent capacity attempting to reconstruct microstructure noise rather than the underlying price dynamics. To resolve this, rigorous denoising is necessary to improve the Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) before feature extraction. Integrating Optuna-optimized wavelet transforms as a pre-processing step to decompose the signals and remove high-frequency irregularities ensures that the autoencoder receives a clean price signal, significantly improving the predictive performance of the downstream neural compression.3. Model Recalibration and Data EngineeringThe transition to intraday execution requires a complete recalibration of the predictive alpha generation layer. Time-series foundation models and state-space filters behave entirely differently when exposed to the heavy-tailed, heteroskedastic nature of intraday market microstructure.3.1 Time-Series Foundation Models in High-Frequency EnvironmentsThe current architecture utilizes Amazon Chronos, a zero-shot time-series foundation model, for directional price forecasting. Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs) are fundamentally designed to treat numerical time-series data akin to language, utilizing transformer architectures to generate probabilistic forecasts. In its original iteration, Chronos processes data by scaling the time-series values and quantizing them into a fixed number of discrete bins, thereby transforming continuous financial data into discrete tokens that a language model can process via cross-entropy loss.While this quantization approach demonstrates robust zero-shot capabilities on daily closing data, its application to 15-minute or hourly intraday bars introduces critical vulnerabilities. Intraday financial data is characterized by intricate, high-order dependencies among Open, High, Low, Close, and Volume (OHLCV) attributes. Quantizing high-frequency, continuous price action into univariate, fixed bins forces the transformer to overfit to transient microstructure noise rather than the underlying price trend. If the binning resolution is too coarse, the model loses the nuanced price dynamics required for 15-minute directional forecasts; if it is too fine, the vocabulary size inflates dramatically, causing the autoregressive computation to capture random noise as deterministic patterns.To mitigate this and successfully deploy foundation models intraday, the architecture must transition either to specialized financial foundation models or adopt continuous patch embeddings.Option A: K-Line Tokenization (The Kronos Architecture)
Research into financial-specific foundation models, such as Kronos, indicates that performance on high-frequency data improves significantly when the tokenization scheme preserves the multi-dimensional relationships between price, volume, and time. Rather than treating a 15-minute bar as a single scalar close price, Kronos abstracts financial K-line (candlestick) sequences as a discrete language. It employs a specialized instance-based tokenizer that discretizes the continuous, multivariate K-line inputs (OHLCV) into a sequence of structured, dual-component (coarse and fine) compact tokens via a learnable codebook. This coarse-to-fine prediction scheme allows the transformer to explicitly model multi-scale market dynamics, capturing both price volatility and volume clustering simultaneously without succumbing to microstructure noise.Option B: Continuous Patch Embeddings (Chronos-2)
Alternatively, adapting the architecture to utilize advanced general models like Chronos-2 eliminates the discrete quantization step entirely. Chronos-2 utilizes continuous patch embeddings—conceptually similar to Vision Transformers (ViT)—grouping consecutive intraday observations into a single "patch" and embedding the entire patch directly as a continuous vector. This circumvents the precision loss and noise-overfitting associated with discrete binning, enabling the model to process 15-minute sequences highly efficiently while retaining the exact continuous price magnitudes. Furthermore, Chronos-2 employs a direct quantile regression head instead of autoregressive token generation, making it vastly superior for probabilistic intraday forecasting. For covariate-informed forecasting, Chronos-2 introduces group attention mechanisms, allowing the model to condition its target asset forecast on the intraday volume profiles or the price action of correlated assets simultaneously within the same context window.3.2 Intraday Signal Decay and Adaptive Kalman FilteringThe current architecture employs a Kalman Filter Agent for dynamic trend-following and mean-reversion signals. On a daily timeframe, linear price impact models and constant measurement noise assumptions are broadly sufficient. However, on an intraday scale, the assumption of homoskedastic (constant) measurement noise collapses. High-frequency price movements are routinely subjected to massive volatility spikes driven by news flow, institutional block trading, and temporary liquidity vacuums in the limit order book.To prevent the Kalman Filter from aggressively updating its state estimation based on pure microstructure noise, the filter must be upgraded to an Adaptive Kalman Filter featuring heteroskedastic measurement noise. The standard state-space representation describes the evolution of the latent informed signal $\theta_t$ and the observed price or normalized order flow $S_t$:$$\theta_t = \phi \theta_{t-1} + \eta_t, \quad \eta_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, Q_t)$$$$S_t = \theta_t + \epsilon_t, \quad \epsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, R_t)$$In a traditional implementation, the measurement noise variance $R_t$ is static. For intraday execution, $R_t$ must be explicitly coupled to realized market volatility. As intraday volatility increases (e.g., during the opening cross or a macroeconomic data release), $R_t$ dynamically inflates. This inflation alters the calculation of the predicted state variance $P_{t\vert{}t-1}$ and reduces the Kalman gain, $K_t$:$$K_t = \frac{P_{t\vert{}t-1}}{P_{t\vert{}t-1} + R_t}$$By reducing the Kalman gain $K_t$ during periods of heightened volatility stress, the filter automatically discounts high-volume panic trading or transient bid-ask spread expansions as noise rather than valid directional signals. This prevents the agent from generating false trend-following signals during chaotic, non-directional intraday churn. Furthermore, empirical studies applying Kalman filtering to intraday data indicate that price impact coefficients and signal informativeness are highly regime-dependent; informed predictive power increases several-fold during crisis periods relative to normal or bull regimes. The Kalman filter should therefore be conditioned on Markov-switching regime probabilities, scaling its output based on the currently identified intraday volatility regime.3.3 Dynamic Ensembling via Non-Stationary Thompson SamplingThe pipeline currently utilizes Thompson Sampling (in thompson_sampling.py) to dynamically adjust the allocation weights given to the Chronos model versus the Kalman and Quant agents based on their out-of-sample profitability. Standard Thompson Sampling is highly effective in stationary environments; however, intraday financial markets are profoundly non-stationary, subject to abrupt intraday regime shifts (e.g., a trending morning session suddenly transitioning into a mean-reverting, low-liquidity lunch session).A standard Thompson Sampling multi-armed bandit maintains a cumulative history of rewards, updating the posterior Beta or Gaussian distributions uniformly. In an intraday setting, retaining old data without decay causes the bandit to react far too slowly to sudden regime shifts, as thousands of historical 15-minute intervals anchor the posterior distribution, preventing the system from reallocating capital to the model currently performing best.To orchestrate the transition to intraday, the ensembling layer must implement Adaptive Discounted Thompson Sampling (ADTS) or Sliding-Window Thompson Sampling (SW-TS). These algorithms forcibly unlearn stale historical performance, ensuring the bandit adapts rapidly to concept drift and abruptly changing environments.For Gaussian reward distributions (representing the continuous PnL generated by the sub-models), the discounted update equations for the empirical mean $\tilde{\mu}$ and the effective observation count $N$ for model $i$ at time $t$, utilizing a discount factor $\gamma \in (0,1)$, are formally defined as:$$\tilde{\mu}_{t+1}(\gamma, i) = \gamma \tilde{\mu}_t(\gamma, i) + \mathbf{1}_{\{i_t = i\}} X_t(i)$$$$N_{t+1}(\gamma, i) = \gamma N_t(\gamma, i) + \mathbf{1}_{\{i_t = i\}}$$$$\hat{\mu}_{t+1}(\gamma, i) = \frac{\tilde{\mu}_{t+1}(\gamma, i)}{N_{t+1}(\gamma, i)}$$The parameter $\theta_t(i)$ for model selection is then sampled from the posterior distribution $\mathcal{N}\left(\hat{\mu}_t(\gamma, i), \frac{4\sigma^2}{N_t(\gamma, i)}\right)$. The discount factor $\gamma$ dictates the half-life of the models' historical performance. An aggressively tuned $\gamma$ (e.g., 0.95) allows the Thompson Sampler to rapidly shift capital allocation from the Kalman agent to the Chronos agent if the market transitions from a trending state to a complex non-linear state mid-day.For portfolio-wide capital allocation across multiple assets, Combinatorial Adaptive Discounted Thompson Sampling (CADTS) can be deployed. CADTS evaluates combinations of weights as "superarms", allowing the system to simultaneously optimize the trust placed in specific predictive agents and the capital distributed across the asset universe, ensuring the total weight sums to one while dynamically adapting to non-stationary intraday conditions.4. Transaction Cost Analysis (TCA) and Overtrading MitigationIncreasing execution frequency from a single daily rebalance to an hourly or 15-minute schedule fundamentally alters the mathematical expectancy of the trading architecture. The primary threat to high-frequency profitability is the continuous crossing of the bid-ask spread and the realization of market impact.4.1 The Exponential Drag of Intraday SlippageIn a daily EOD trading system, the predicted alpha of a rebalancing signal must only exceed a single instance of transaction costs. In a 15-minute execution model, minor fluctuations in model predictions can induce continuous, marginal adjustments to portfolio weights. Every adjustment incurs fixed transaction costs (brokerage fees) and variable transaction costs (slippage and the bid-ask spread).If the system blindly follows the raw, unpenalized output of the Thompson Sampling layer 26 times a day, the continuous portfolio turnover will rapidly erode the expected value of the strategy. The slippage—the deviation between the intended mid-price and the actual filled price—becomes the dominant factor in the strategy's PnL. Research indicates that roughly 90% of intraday trading strategies fail because high trade frequency compounds costs; the transaction load quietly erodes the edge through spreads, slippage, and execution variance. To preserve the structural edge, the portfolio construction logic within the Governance Firewall must be mathematically upgraded to include a severe transaction cost penalty, ensuring trades are only routed when the expected alpha significantly outweighs the friction of execution.4.2 Modifying the Governance Firewall: The Mathematical No-Trade RegionMost rudimentary portfolio optimization techniques ignore transaction costs at the selection stage and merely account for them post-execution, leading to suboptimal, high-turnover behavior. The upgraded firewall.py must integrate transaction costs directly into the mean-variance objective function, forcing greater precision in the choice of scale and the estimated alphas.Let $w$ represent the proposed target portfolio weights, $\tilde{w}$ represent the current existing portfolio weights, $\mu$ the vector of expected intraday returns generated by the alpha engines, $\Sigma$ the covariance matrix, and $\gamma$ the risk aversion parameter. The standard mean-variance optimization must be augmented with a penalty function representing the cost of rebalancing from $\tilde{w}$ to $w$. The optimization problem is formulated as:$$w^* = \arg\max_{w} \left( w^T \mu - \frac{\gamma}{2} w^T \Sigma w - C(w \mid \tilde{w}) \right)$$where $C(w \mid \tilde{w})$ models the transaction costs. The specification of this cost function dictates the trading behavior of the intraday system:Proportional Costs (The $L_1$ Penalty): To model the bid-ask spread and flat brokerage fees, the cost function takes a linear, absolute-value form: $\kappa \Vert{} \Lambda (w - \tilde{w}) \Vert{}_1$, where $\kappa$ is the transaction cost parameter and $\Lambda$ scales for specific asset liquidity.Quadratic Costs (The $L_2$ Penalty): To model the market impact of larger orders eating into the limit order book, the cost function takes a quadratic form: $\kappa (w - \tilde{w})^T \Lambda (w - \tilde{w})$.Integrating the $L_1$ proportional cost penalty into the optimizer induces a critical phenomenon known as the "No-Trade Region" (NTR). The NTR is a mathematically defined multidimensional space around the current portfolio weights $\tilde{w}$.When the alpha generators suggest a new set of optimal weights $w_{\text{ideal}}$, the Firewall computes whether $w_{\text{ideal}}$ falls within the current NTR. If it does, the predicted alpha is insufficient to overcome the hurdle rate imposed by the bid-ask spread, and the optimizer dictates a zero-trade policy, keeping $w = \tilde{w}$. If the ideal weights fall significantly outside the NTR, the system does not trade all the way to $w_{\text{ideal}}$. Instead, it trades only exactly to the boundary of the NTR, minimizing transaction costs while restoring the portfolio to an acceptable risk-reward threshold.By embedding this $L_1$ constrained quadratic programming problem (solvable via interior point methods in libraries like cvxpy) into the Firewall, the architecture naturally self-regulates its execution frequency. During periods of low intraday signal conviction, the system will hold positions entirely static for hours. It will only cross the spread when the Chronos or Kalman agents forecast a sharp, high-magnitude dislocation that vastly exceeds the dynamically measured intraday spread.5. Intraday Risk Guardrails: Adapting the Volatility GuardThe current system calculates a daily asymmetric GJR-GARCH forecast to set dynamic Take-Profit (TP) and Stop-Loss (SL) brackets in volatility_guard.py. Attempting to run a standard GARCH(1,1) or GJR-GARCH model on 15-minute intraday data will yield severely distorted forecasts, as standard volatility models inherently assume the unconditional variance is constant over time.5.1 The Diurnal Pattern of Intraday VolatilityIntraday financial data is characterized by a pronounced deterministic seasonality known as the diurnal pattern, typically manifesting as a "U-shape" or reverse "J-shape". Volatility is exceptionally high during the opening minutes of the cash session as overnight information is assimilated and rapid price discovery occurs. Volatility then decays significantly during the mid-day lunch hours, and surges again into the market close as institutional portfolios execute market-on-close orders and rebalance.If a standard GJR-GARCH model is fed 15-minute data, it will misinterpret the morning volatility spike as a persistent stochastic shock and erroneously forecast massive, persisting volatility for the quiet mid-day period. Conversely, it will fail to anticipate the systemic closing spike. Therefore, the Volatility Guard must be entirely rewritten to decouple deterministic time-of-day volatility from true stochastic market shocks.5.2 The Multiplicative Component GARCH (MC-GARCH)To resolve this, the architecture must implement the Multiplicative Component GARCH (MC-GARCH) framework pioneered by Engle and Sokalska. The MC-GARCH model decomposes the conditional variance of high-frequency returns into three distinct, easily interpretable multiplicative components:$$r_{t,n} = \sqrt{h_t s_n q_{t,n}} \epsilon_{t,n}, \quad \epsilon_{t,n} \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$$Where:$h_t$ is the daily variance component, representing the macro volatility regime of day $t$. This can be forecasted using a standard daily asymmetric EGARCH or GARCH model on EOD data.$s_n$ is the diurnal (seasonal) variance component for the $n$-th intraday bin (e.g., the 10:00 AM - 10:15 AM period). It is calculated non-parametrically as the historical average of the variance of returns in that specific time bin across previous days, deflated by the daily variance. Advanced implementations utilize a piecewise linear structure or spline function to smoothly model this periodicity.$q_{t,n}$ is the stochastic intraday variance component. This captures the actual unexpected shocks remaining after stripping away the daily macro regime and the time-of-day expectation.By normalizing the raw 15-minute returns by the daily and diurnal components ($z_{t,n} = r_{t,n} / \sqrt{h_t s_n}$), the resulting time series contains only the true, unexpected intraday shocks. The system then fits a unit GJR-GARCH(1,1) model to this normalized series to forecast $q_{t,n+1}$:$$q_{t,n+1} = \omega + \alpha z_{t,n}^2 + \gamma \mathbf{1}_{\{z_{t,n} < 0\}} z_{t,n}^2 + \beta q_{t,n}$$where the indicator function $\mathbf{1}_{\{z_{t,n} < 0\}}$ captures the leverage effect, ensuring that sudden intraday sell-offs trigger wider volatility forecasts than equal-sized rallies, a fundamental asymmetry in equity markets.5.3 Dynamic Intraday OCO BracketsThe recombined forecast $\hat{\sigma}^2_{t,n+1} = \hat{h}_t \hat{s}_{n+1} \hat{q}_{t,n+1}$ provides a highly accurate, time-adjusted projection of volatility for the next 15-minute bar. The Alpaca Order Management script will utilize this value to dynamically set trailing OCO (One-Cancels-Other) brackets.During the volatile morning session, the diurnal component $\hat{s}_{n}$ is large, naturally widening the stop-loss distances to prevent the algorithm from being prematurely stopped out by normal opening fluctuations and slippage. As the market enters the mid-day lull, $\hat{s}_{n}$ shrinks mathematically, allowing the system to mechanically tighten the stop-loss and take-profit brackets. This protects capital against sudden, low-liquidity flash crashes while minimizing the capital locked in unnecessarily wide margin parameters during slow periods.6. Managing Overnight Gap Risk: Expectancy and Strategic AlternativesThe most profound dilemma in intraday trading architecture is the management of open positions at the close of the daily cash session. Financial markets continue to price in macroeconomic data, geopolitical events, and corporate earnings while primary exchanges are closed, resulting in the overnight gap upon the next open.6.1 The Mathematics of Gap Risk and Stop-Loss FailureHolding positions overnight exposes the portfolio to severe left-tail risk because traditional risk management tools fail mechanically during market closures. A stop-loss order placed at a 2% drawdown is entirely ineffective if the market opens with a 5% gap down; the order will execute as a market order at the first available open price, resulting in massive execution slippage that vastly exceeds the mathematical variance allowed by the portfolio design.A strategy's mathematical expectancy is highly sensitive to the asymmetry of losses. If an intraday system is calibrated with a fixed reward-to-risk ratio, a single overnight gap that generates an oversized loss destroys the risk distribution, requiring an exponentially higher win rate simply to return the portfolio to breakeven.6.2 Strategic Comparison: Complete Liquidation vs. Bracket TighteningThe architecture faces two primary methodologies for handling the EOD transition, each with distinct mathematical expectancy profiles.Approach A: Complete Daily Liquidation (Zero Overnight Risk)
The system forces a complete liquidation of all active positions 15 to 30 minutes prior to the market close, flattening the book.Advantage: Absolute elimination of overnight gap risk. Capital is preserved in fiat, entirely insulating the portfolio from after-hours exogenous shocks and earnings announcements.Disadvantage: It forfeits the "Liquidity-of-Time Premium" (TLP) and the overnight risk premium—a documented phenomenon where a substantial portion of long-term equity returns is generated while the market is closed. Furthermore, it strictly enforces two transaction costs per trade cycle (closing at 3:45 PM, and re-entering at 9:30 AM the next day if the predictive signal persists).Approach B: EOD Bracket Tightening (Managed Gap Exposure)
The system retains the positions but uses the final 15 minutes of the trading session to dynamically tighten the OCO brackets based on the forecasted overnight variance.Advantage: Captures the overnight risk premium and avoids the bid-ask spread crossing costs of forced EOD liquidation.Disadvantage: The portfolio remains exposed to extreme gaps that leap over the tightened stop-loss, realizing severe slippage upon the morning open.6.3 Expectancy Modeling for the EOD DecisionTo programmatically choose between these states rather than relying on a static rule, the Governance Firewall must compute a daily comparative expectancy model at 3:45 PM.Let $E[\text{Hold}]$ represent the expected value of holding the asset overnight. This is a function of the model's directional conviction (derived from the Chronos and Kalman models) and the historical probability of an overnight gap exceeding the target threshold. Let $c_{\text{spread}}$ represent the total round-trip transaction costs of liquidating and re-entering the following morning.The system should hold the position overnight only if the expected overnight alpha, discounted by the probability and magnitude of a tail-risk gap down, strictly exceeds the known cost of liquidation:$$E[\text{Hold}] - \left( P(\text{Gap} < \text{Stop}) \times E[\text{Gap Loss}] \right) > - c_{\text{spread}}$$If the transaction costs of crossing the spread at the close and the open are extremely high (e.g., illiquid mid-cap stocks), the model mathematically favors holding and tightening stops. However, for highly liquid assets (e.g., S&P 500 ETFs or major FX pairs) where $c_{\text{spread}}$ is negligible (often 1-2 basis points), empirical evidence dictates that optimal stopping theory frequently triggers liquidation before the close when uncertainty is high, as the cost of liquidating is cheaper than absorbing the variance of the overnight gap. The architecture should programmatically evaluate this inequality on an asset-by-asset basis 15 minutes before the close.7. This bridges the gap between daily and high-frequency trading by linking the execution frequency directly to the presence of cost-adjusted structural edge.8. SynthesisTransitioning an AI-driven trading architecture from a daily EOD schedule to an intraday schedule requires deep structural modifications to account for microstructure noise, non-stationarity, and transaction costs.To prevent foundational models like Amazon Chronos and the Conditional Autoencoder from overfitting to transient microstructure noise, data must be denoised via wavelet transforms, and tokenization strategies must utilize continuous patch embeddings or specialized OHLCV schemas. State-space models and bandit ensemble algorithms must be upgraded to feature heteroskedastic measurement noise and Adaptive Discounted Thompson Sampling (ADTS), ensuring the system can rapidly discount stale information and adapt to intraday regime shifts.Most critically, the viability of the transition hinges entirely on the mastery of friction. An $L_1$ transaction cost penalty must be embedded directly into the mean-variance optimizer to enforce a mathematical No-Trade Region, protecting the system from the exponential drag of intraday slippage. Finally, by deploying a Multiplicative Component GJR-GARCH model to map the diurnal volatility smile, the system can dynamically size OCO brackets, providing the mechanical foundation needed to calculate the precise threshold where holding an asset overnight becomes mathematically inferior to forced EOD liquidation. Implementing these architectures will yield a highly responsive, cost-aware intraday engine capable of navigating continuous market dynamics without falling victim to overtrading.
